## The finding that is about process, not code

`"every resolver test needs a decoy"` had already been promoted to the decision log. I applied it to the three cases review #4 named — relative imports, SELF_FIELD, inheritance — and to nothing else. Aliases, workspace packages, field conflicts, and star re-exports all shipped decoy-free.

**All four hid a bug.** Four confident wrong-`RESOLVED` edges were live while all 34 tests passed.

A rule applied only to the instances someone else identified is not a discipline. The generalisation, now recorded: *when a promoted decision names a class of test, it applies to every new surface in that class, not to the examples that produced it.*

## The observation I cannot fix with code

> "The scorecard measures pinning, not correctness. A wrong edge counts as RESOLVED."

Structurally true, and worth stating plainly rather than engineering around. The percentage cannot distinguish a correct resolution from a confident wrong one. Only two things can: **decoy fixtures**, and **spot-audits of real-repo edges** against known-correct targets.

That is why all twelve regression tests from this review are decoys, and why the earlier flask verification checked that specific imports landed on specific files rather than trusting the percentage that had just improved.

---

## Contracts P1-3 and later must know

1. IMPORTS edges may carry `("type_only", "true")`; these are not runtime dependencies and must not be treated as such in flow or impact analysis.
2. A barrel import emits **two** edges: file-level IMPORTS to the barrel, symbol-level REFERENCES to the definition. Module aggregation must not double-count.
3. `FileFacts.lang` is `"typescript"` even for `.js` files, so there is no separate `javascript` scorecard row.
4. Candidate arity is capped at 8; call edges carry a `shape` attribute including `self_field`.

---

## Riskiest remaining untested assumption

That the headline numbers measure what they imply. 99.9% is the **imports** bin on one repo; correctness of the resolved edges behind it has been spot-audited on flask but not on the TypeScript subjects. A wrong edge counts as RESOLVED by construction.
